\begin{abstract}
These reading notes reorganize the OpenAI eventual categoricity
manuscript around a sequence of notions of type: presentation
records and order types, automorphism orbits, arrow types, Galois
types, and natural or placed canonical diagrams. We emphasize the
role of EM functoriality and carefully separate equalities that
are proved, implications that are only one-way, and analogies
with existing AEC technology. The final sections record locality,
compactness, amalgamation at closed levels, and the first
definitions for finite systems. This is not an independent
verification of the manuscript.
\end{abstract}

\section{Strategy and notation}
Fix an AEC $(\K,\leq_\K)$ categorical in unboundedly many cardinals.
Write $\lambda_0=\LS(\K)$; use $\lambda,\kappa,\mu$ for infinite
cardinals and $\ell(\ba)$ only for the length of a tuple.
Fix an EM template $\Phi$ in $\tau\supseteq\tau(\K)$.
Unless otherwise stated, $\EM(I,\Phi)$ denotes its reduct
to $\tau(\K)$.

The argument moves from order types and EM records through
automorphism orbits, arrow comparisons and Galois types,

\begin{remark}[Notation crosswalk]
The earlier strong type under skeleton-induced automorphisms
becomes $\EMauttp$. The earlier canonical diagram is the
stabilized arrow type $\atp$. The ordinary unrestricted
Galois type $\gtp$ can be strictly coarser than
$\atp$ in the absence of amalgamation
(Example~\ref{ex:stars-gtp-arrow}). The source's stable diagrams
means \emph{stabilized} rather than stable in
the AEC sense. Our $\natatp$ is precisely the
source's natural diagram $D_{\mathrm{nat}}$;
we change the name to emphasize that it is
computed from stabilized arrow types. We use $\gtp^{\mathrm{EM}}$ only after
defining it, not as an automatic synonym for $\natatp$.
\end{remark}

\section{Types of types}\label{sec:types}
We organize the arguments of the source's Sections 3--4 by
the kinds of type that are compared. The class
$\Kord$ of linear orders with order embeddings
provides the index-side invariants; the EM functor
lifts order automorphisms, embeddings, and amalgams
to $\K$. These give different comparisons of the
evaluated tuples.

\subsection{Presentation records and order types}\label{sec:records}
\begin{definition}[Presentation record]\label{def:records}
Let $I$ be a linear order. For an output ordinal
$\gamma$, a \emph{presentation record from $I$ in $\tau$}
is a pair $r=(\bar f,\bi)$, where
\[
\bar f=(f_k)_{k<\gamma},\qquad
\bi=(i_j)_{j<\alpha}
\]
is strictly increasing in $I$, and every $f_k$ is
a $\tau$-term in the variables $(x_j)_{j<\alpha}$
using only finitely many variables. Discarding
unused indices gives a canonical support convention.
The collection $\Rec^\gamma_\tau(I,\Phi)$ of all such
records of fixed output length $\gamma$ is a set;
so is $\Rec^{\leq\theta}_\tau(I,\Phi)$ for any
fixed cardinal $\theta$. The union over all
possible output ordinals would not be a set.

The \emph{evaluation map} is
\[
\operatorname{ev}_I^\gamma:
\Rec^\gamma_\tau(I,\Phi)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{EM}_\tau(I,\Phi)^\gamma,\qquad
(\bar f,\bi)\longmapsto (f_k(\bi))_{k<\gamma}.
\]
A record $r$ is a presentation record \emph{of}
$\ba$ if $\operatorname{ev}_I^\gamma(r)=\ba$.
Every tuple has one by the EM finite-support
property, but it need not be unique.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Order types]\label{def:ordtypes}
Let $\bi$ be a sequence from a linear order $I$.
Its \emph{quantifier-free order type}
$\operatorname{tp}(\bi;I)$ records all order and
equality relations among its coordinates.
The augmented order type
$\tpstar(\bi;I)$ also records whether each
strict slot over the distinct coordinates
of $\bi$ is occupied (including the two end slots).
For example, where $x<y$ are consecutive
distinct entries, it records the truth of
$\exists z\,(x<z<y)$; for infinite tuples this
also includes cuts not necessarily specified by
a single pair of consecutive entries.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Automorphism types}\label{subsec:auto}
\begin{definition}[Automorphism orbit type]
For any structure $M$ and tuple $\ba\in M^\gamma$,
\[
\auttp(\ba;M)=[\ba]_{\operatorname{Aut}(M)}
\]
is the orbit of $\ba$ under the full automorphism
group of $M$. This depends on the ambient model.
It is especially useful in homogeneous structures
and monster models. For a chosen EM presentation,
the subgroup induced by order automorphisms gives
the potentially finer $\EMauttp$ defined next.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The homogeneous skeletons]
The source constructs
$\Gamma_\delta=\mathbb Z^{(\delta)}$ and
$H(\delta)=\mathbb Q^{(\Gamma_\delta)}$,
using finitely supported sequences and the prescribed
reverse-lexicographic orders. For infinite limit
$\delta$ these orders have controlled orbits of
short tuples; the key invariant is $\tpstar$,
not merely $\operatorname{tp}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Order automorphism classification {\cite[Theorem 3.5]{oai-ecc}}]\label{thm:order-orbits} For tuples $\bi,\bj$ of length at most $\theta$, where
$\delta$ is an infinite limit ordinal,
$\theta$ is infinite, and $\theta<\operatorname{cf}(\delta)$,
\[
\tpstar(\bi;H(\delta))
=\tpstar(\bj;H(\delta))
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists h\in\operatorname{Aut}(H(\delta))\;
\bigl(h(\bi)=\bj\bigr).
\]
In particular, these order types classify the automorphism orbits of
tuples in $H(\delta)$. For bounded tuple lengths the number of such types
is bounded independently of $\delta$.
\end{theorem}


\begin{definition}[EM-induced automorphism types]
Fix an infinite cardinal $\theta$ and suppose $\operatorname{cf}(\delta)>\theta$.
Write $M_\delta=\EM(H(\delta),\Phi)$ for the $\tau(\K)$-reduct,
with its specified expanded EM presentation understood.
Every order automorphism $h\in\operatorname{Aut}(H(\delta))$
induces a uniquely specified automorphism
$\widehat h=\EM(h,\Phi)\in\operatorname{Aut}(M_\delta)$
by functoriality, acting on terms by
\[
\widehat h\bigl(f(\bi)\bigr)=f\bigl(h(\bi)\bigr).
\]
For $\theta$-tuples $\ba,\bb\in M_\delta$, define
\[
\ba\equiv^{\mathrm{EM}}_{\mathrm{aut}}\bb
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists h\in\operatorname{Aut}(H(\delta))\
\bigl(\widehat h(\ba)=\bb\bigr).
\]
This is an equivalence relation, as the induced maps form a group
action. Define
\[
\EMauttp(\ba;M_\delta):=[\ba]_{\equiv^{\mathrm{EM}}_{\mathrm{aut}}},
\qquad
S^{\mathrm{EM,aut}}_\delta:=
\bigl\{\EMauttp(\ba;M_\delta):\ba\in M_\delta^\theta\bigr\}.
\]
We suppress $\theta$ and $\Phi$ in $S^{\mathrm{EM,aut}}_\delta$.
The induced lift $\widehat h$ is canonical for the fixed EM
presentation; the definition is \emph{relative to that presentation}
and need not be intrinsic to an abstract copy of the AEC model.
In particular, this equivalence can be finer than the full
automorphism-orbit equivalence of $M_\delta$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Equal occupied-slot records imply equal EM-automorphism type]
Suppose $\ba,\bb\in M_\delta^\theta$ admit presentation records in common.
Then $\ba\equiv^{\mathrm{EM}}_{\mathrm{aut}}\bb$.

\begin{proof}\itshape 
Choose presentations
\[
\ba=(f_k(\bi):k<\theta),\qquad
\bb=(f_k(\bj):k<\theta)
\]
with the same term list and
$\tpstar(\bi;H(\delta))
 =\tpstar(\bj;H(\delta))$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:order-orbits}, since the flattened index lists have length at
most $\theta<\operatorname{cf}(\delta)$, there is
$h\in\operatorname{Aut}(H(\delta))$ with $h(\bi)=\bj$.
Functoriality gives
\[
\widehat h(\ba)
 =\bigl(f_k(h(\bi)):k<\theta\bigr)
 =\bigl(f_k(\bj):k<\theta\bigr)=\bb.
\]
Thus $\ba\equiv^{\mathrm{EM}}_{\mathrm{aut}}\bb$.
\end{proof}
\end{lemma}

\begin{remark}[Records and orbit bounds]
The converse is not asserted: different term presentations, even of
the same tuple, may give different records. This lemma is the
direction needed to bound the number of EM-induced automorphism types by the number
of possible records. The manuscript's Lemma~3.6 establishes a
uniform bound on \emph{full model-automorphism orbits}; the present
EM-induced types are orbits under the subgroup induced from the index
order, and the same record bound applies when
$\operatorname{cf}(\delta)>\theta$.
\end{remark}


\begin{definition}[The arrow relation]
For $p,q\in S^{\mathrm{EM,aut}}_\delta$, write
\[
p\longrightarrow q
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{gathered}
\exists\ba\models p\;\exists\bb\models q\;
\exists f:M_\delta\longrightarrow M_\delta\\
\text{$f$ a strong embedding of the AEC reduct and }f(\ba)=\bb.
\end{gathered}
\]
This relation is well-defined on the EM-induced automorphism types: changes of
representatives are effected by induced automorphisms, which are
strong automorphisms of the reduct. It is reflexive and transitive;
symmetry is the eventual reversal phenomenon discussed below.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{The strong-map preorder and presentation profiles.}
Here the arrow notation is also used for the related but
\emph{different} orbit space of an AEC model; this does not
identify fixed-skeleton strong types with all model orbits.
At a categoricity cardinal $\kappa$, fix a model $M_\kappa$ of size $\kappa$. Distinct from the induced-order-automorphism types $S^{\mathrm{EM,aut}}_\delta$ just defined, for automorphism orbits $p,r$ of tuples of length at most $\theta$, write
\[
p\longrightarrow r
\]
if a strong self-embedding of $M_\kappa$ sends a representative of $p$ to a representative of $r$. This relation is reflexive and transitive, but symmetry is not automatic.

The paper names the boundedly many full automorphism orbits via maps $G_\kappa$ and assigns orbit names to presentation records via $F_{\kappa,\delta}$. A presentation \emph{profile} records (i) which records realize which orbit names and (ii) the arrow relation between named orbits. There are boundedly many such profiles.

Lemmas 3.7, 3.8, and 3.10 provide order/ordinal bookkeeping; Proposition 3.9 is the important alignment statement allowing small and large presentations to have matching profile data.


\subsubsection*{Eventual reversal}
\begin{theorem}[Eventual reversal {\cite[Theorem 3.11]{oai-ecc}}]\label{thm:reversal}
For every infinite cardinal $\theta$ there is a cardinal $b_\K(\theta)$ such that, whenever $\kappa\geq b_\K(\theta)$ is a categoricity cardinal, $M\in\K$ has size $\kappa$, $f:M\to M$ is strong, and $\bar a$ has length at most $\theta$, there is a strong $g:M\to M$ satisfying
\[
g(f(a_i))=a_i\qquad\text{for every coordinate }i.
\]
Equivalently, $\longrightarrow$ becomes symmetric on bounded tuple orbits at sufficiently large categoricity cardinals.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]\itshape  If asymmetry persisted unboundedly, one could choose repeated profiles at separated categoricity sizes, align small submodels with large presentations, and carry out an elimination construction. The resulting categoricity model would omit the ability to reach a chosen orbit, contradicting the presence of that orbit in the unique model.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\emph{Interpretation.} Strong self-embeddings become reversible \emph{on prescribed bounded tuples}; the theorem does not say that every strong self-embedding is an automorphism. This is the first major new structural theorem in our reading.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Eventual reversal as a substitute for homogeneity]
\label{rem:reversal-homogeneity}
One of the striking features of Theorem~\ref{thm:reversal}
is that it provides a \emph{local substitute for homogeneity
before amalgamation is known}. Fix a bound $\theta$ on tuple
length, and let $M$ be the unique model at a sufficiently
large categoricity cardinal. A strong self-embedding
$f:M\longrightarrow M$ need not be onto, and the theorem
does not extend $f^{-1}$ to an automorphism. Nevertheless,
for every $\ba\in M^{\leq\theta}$ there is another strong
self-embedding $g:M\longrightarrow M$ with
\[
g(f(\ba))=\ba.
\]
Thus the directed preorder on the \emph{full automorphism
orbits} of short tuples, induced by strong self-embeddings,
becomes symmetric at those cardinals. This supplies
an equivalence relation of arrow types without assuming
the amalgamation property or first constructing a
monster model.

For comparison, in an AEC with amalgamation and a suitably
homogeneous monster model $\mathfrak C$, equality of Galois
types can be described by automorphism orbits in
$\mathfrak C$. The resulting automorphism of the
\emph{monster}, however, need not restrict to a strong
self-embedding of a specified smaller model $M$.
Likewise, amalgamation by itself does not guarantee
reversal inside that $M$. Theorem~\ref{thm:reversal}
is a statement about the categoricity model itself,
not simply the familiar monster-model interpretation
of Galois types.

The proof then uses a uniform bound on these orbit
comparisons to establish stabilization
(source Proposition~4.2). Schematically,
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{unbounded categoricity}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{local reversal of strong embeddings}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{stabilized arrow types}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{placed comparisons and locality/compactness}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{amalgamation at selected cardinals}.
\end{gathered}
\]
The unusual direction is important: rather than
assuming amalgamation to obtain a useful type
calculus, the manuscript builds the type calculus
\emph{in order to establish amalgamation}. Even
after reversal, unrestricted Galois-type equality
need not coincide with equality of arrow types:
the star example (Example~\ref{ex:stars-gtp-arrow})
shows that Galois-equivalence chains can pass through
smaller intermediate models.

\emph{Question for later.} Can a model-theoretic
hypothesis weaker than unbounded categoricity,
perhaps an appropriate superstability or homogeneity
condition, imply bounded-tuple reversal of strong
embeddings in a useful class of models? If so,
might one recover a stabilized arrow-type calculus
without requiring a monster model as an initial
hypothesis? This is a proposed direction for
investigation, not a theorem of the source.
\end{remark}


\subsection{Arrow types}\label{subsec:arrow}
Fix an infinite tuple-length bound $\theta$ and a categoricity
cardinal $\kappa\geq\lambda_0+\theta$ above the reversal
threshold $b_\K(\theta)$ in the definitions below.
The existence of a strong embedding carrying one tuple
to another is initially directed. The eventual reversal
theorem makes it an equivalence relation at sufficiently
large categoricity sizes. Its classes are \emph{arrow types}.
The stabilized arrow type is precisely what the source
calls a \emph{canonical comparison diagram}.

\begin{definition}[Comparison at size $\kappa$]
For $M,N\in\K_\kappa$ and tuples $\ba\in M^\theta$, $\bb\in N^\theta$, write
\[
(\ba;M)\equiv^{*,\theta}_\kappa(\bb;N)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists f:M\longrightarrow N\text{ strong with }f(\ba)=\bb.
\]
Under the stated hypotheses this is an equivalence relation: reflexivity and transitivity are immediate. For symmetry, use categoricity to identify $M$ and $N$ with the same $\kappa$-sized model and apply Theorem~\ref{thm:reversal} to the resulting strong self-embedding and the given tuple.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Comparison in larger models and arrow types]
For $M,N\in\K_{\geq\kappa}$ and $\theta$-tuples $\ba\in M$, $\bb\in N$, write
\[
(\ba;M)\equiv^\theta_\kappa(\bb;N)
\]
if there exist $M_0\preccurlyeq_\K M$, $N_0\preccurlyeq_\K N$ of cardinality $\kappa$, with $\ba\in M_0^\theta$ and $\bb\in N_0^\theta$, such that
\[
(\ba;M_0)\equiv^{*,\theta}_\kappa(\bb;N_0).
\]
\par
For a tuple $\ba\in M^\gamma$, define its
\emph{arrow type at $\kappa$} by
\[
\atp_\kappa(\ba;M):=[(\ba;M)]_{\equiv_\kappa^\gamma}.
\]
The parameter $\kappa$ refers to the size of the strong
submodels used for comparison, not necessarily $|M|$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Independence of small submodel {\cite[Lemma 4.1]{oai-ecc}}]\label{lem:small}
If $M_0,M_1\preccurlyeq_\K M$ have cardinality $\kappa$ and both contain $\ba$, then
\[
(\ba;M_0)\equiv^{*,\theta}_\kappa(\ba;M_1).
\]
Indeed, by downward L\"owenheim--Skolem choose a $\kappa$-sized $M_*\preccurlyeq_\K M$ containing $M_0\cup M_1$. Coherence yields $M_i\preccurlyeq_\K M_*$ for $i=0,1$. The inclusions witness
$(\ba;M_i)\equiv^{*,\theta}_\kappa(\ba;M_*)$; symmetry and transitivity give the claim.
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}
The relation $\equiv^\theta_\kappa$ is an equivalence relation on pairs $(\ba;M)$ with $M\in\K_{\geq\kappa}$, and it is independent of which $\kappa$-sized strong submodels containing the tuples are chosen. For transitivity, replace the two intermediate $\kappa$-submodels inside the middle model by equivalent ones using the lemma, then compose the resulting strong maps at size $\kappa$. The relation is preserved by strong embeddings of the ambient models.
\end{corollary}

\begin{definition}[Type sets]
For $\gamma\leq\theta$, set
\[
S^\gamma_\kappa:=\bigl\{[(\ba;M)]_{\equiv^\gamma_\kappa}: M\in\K_{\geq\kappa},\ \ba\in M^\gamma\bigr\},
\qquad
S^{\leq\theta}_\kappa:=\coprod_{\gamma\leq\theta}S^\gamma_\kappa.
\]
By the small-submodel lemma every class has a representative in a $\kappa$-sized model. Categoricity identifies these with the quotient of $M_\kappa^\gamma$ by $\equiv^{*,\gamma}_\kappa$, for any fixed $M_\kappa\in\K_\kappa$, so these are sets (up to the usual coding of representatives). The uniform orbit bound controls their cardinalities at sufficiently large categoricity cardinals. The superscript $\gamma$ denotes the \emph{exact} tuple length.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Arrow types as relative Galois equivalence]
The relation $\equiv_\kappa^\gamma$ compares
tuples inside $\kappa$-sized strong submodels,
even when ambient models are larger.
It is the Galois-type equivalence relation
\emph{restricted to chains through models of size
at least $\kappa$}, as made precise in
Proposition~\ref{prop:arrow-galois} below.
It need not coincide with the usual unrestricted
Galois equivalence in the absence of amalgamation.
Stabilization of these restricted classes is
distinct from AEC stability (a bound on types
over a model).
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{Stabilization of arrow types}
\begin{proposition}[Stabilization {\cite[Proposition 4.2]{oai-ecc}}]
For categoricity cardinals $\lambda\geq\kappa$ above the reversal thresholds, restriction to $\kappa$-sized strong submodels gives projections, for each fixed tuple length $\gamma\leq\sigma$,
\[
\pi^{\gamma}_{\lambda\kappa}:S^\gamma_\lambda
\longrightarrow S^\gamma_\kappa,
\qquad
[(M,\bar a)]\longmapsto[(M_0,\bar a)],
\]
where $M_0\leq_\K M$ has cardinality $\kappa$ and contains the tuple. Lemma~\ref{lem:small} makes this independent of the choice of $M_0$. Taking the disjoint union gives
$\pi^{\leq\sigma}_{\lambda\kappa}:S^{\leq\sigma}_\lambda\to S^{\leq\sigma}_\kappa$.
These maps compose and are surjective: an EM presentation $\EM(I,\Phi)$ at size $\kappa$ can be extended, by extending $I$, to a presentation $\EM(J,\Phi)$ at size $\lambda$ (with the AEC reduct understood).

More precisely, there is a categoricity cardinal $c_\sigma$ above the
$\sigma$-tuple reversal threshold such that for every pair of categoricity
cardinals $c_\sigma\leq\kappa\leq\lambda$, the projection
$\pi^{\leq\sigma}_{\lambda\kappa}$ is bijective. This is
\emph{stabilization of the comparison classes}, not model-theoretic
stability. 
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]\itshape 
Suppose that noninjective projections occur
arbitrarily late. Choose $B_\sigma^+$ successive refinement stages and a
common categoricity cardinal above them. Each refinement produces a pair
of classes in the final model agreeing at an earlier stage but distinct
at a later one. Pairs arising at different stages are distinct, because
separation persists under further projection. Yet the final class set has
size at most the infinite cardinal $B_\sigma$, and hence has at most
$B_\sigma^2=B_\sigma$ ordered pairs, a contradiction.


\end{proof}
\begin{definition}[Stabilized arrow type (canonical diagram); extracted from
{\cite[Proposition 4.2]{oai-ecc}}]
Fix an infinite cardinal $\sigma$ and a categoricity cardinal
$c_\sigma$ beyond the stabilization and reversal thresholds for
tuples of length at most $\sigma$. For $M\in\K_{\geq c_\sigma}$
and $\bar a\in M^\gamma$, $\gamma\leq\sigma$, define
\[
\atp(\bar a;M)
 :=[(M_0,\bar a)]_{\equiv^{\gamma}_{c_\sigma}},
\]
where $M_0\leq_\K M$ has size $c_\sigma$ and contains $\bar a$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:small} makes the definition independent of $M_0$.
Stabilization makes equality of these diagrams independent of the
chosen sufficiently large categoricity cardinal. We also write $D_\sigma(\bar a;M)$ or $D(\bar a;M)$
when quoting the source. Thus the stabilized arrow type
$\atp$ is precisely its canonical comparison diagram.
\end{definition}

Proposition~4.2 of the source manuscript further establishes:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Equality of diagrams is preserved by strong embeddings; there are at most $B_\sigma$ diagrams of the prescribed bounded lengths.
\item Diagrams contain atomic information and respect restriction, reindexing, and enlargement of the tuple bound.
\item At categoricity cardinals at least the paper's nondecreasing threshold $t(\sigma)$, equality of diagrams is implemented by a strong map.
\item Compatible joint diagrams can be transported over any realization of the relevant initial diagram in a sufficiently large categoricity model.
\end{enumerate}
The paper chooses $t(\sigma)$ above the stabilization thresholds
$c_\tau$ for every infinite $\tau\leq\sigma$; no explicit numerical
formula for $c_\sigma$ is supplied.

The canonical diagram is \emph{not} simply a presentation record from the order classification (Theorem~\ref{thm:order-orbits}). Records depend on chosen EM coordinates and give sufficient conditions for orbit equivalence; stabilized strong-map classes are intrinsic to the AEC.


\subsection{Galois types and EM-lifted comparisons}\label{subsec:galois}
The standard AEC Galois type is obtained from joint
embedding of tuples, not directly from the choice
of a particular EM presentation. Without amalgamation,
we use the equivalence relation generated by direct
joint-embedding comparisons.

\begin{definition}[Galois types over the empty set]
For pairs $(\ba;M)$ and $(\bb;N)$ of equal tuple length, say
they are \emph{directly equivalent by amalgamation} if there are strong embeddings
$f_0:M\to P$ and $f_1:N\to P$ with $f_0(\ba)=f_1(\bb)$.
Without assuming amalgamation, define equality of
$\operatorname{gtp}(\ba/\emptyset;M)$ and
$\operatorname{gtp}(\bb/\emptyset;N)$ as the equivalence relation
generated by direct equivalence (its transitive closure).
This is distinct from the atomic diagram of a tuple and
makes sense without assuming the amalgamation property.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Galois types over a model]
More generally, for $A\leq_\K M$ and $\ba\in M^\gamma$,
the \emph{Galois type of $\ba$ over $A$ in $M$},
$\gtp(\ba/A;M)$, is the equivalence class generated by
direct identifications of tuples in strong amalgams
\emph{over the same copy of $A$}. Thus
$(\ba;M)$ and $(\bb;N)$ are directly equivalent
over $A$ if $A\leq_\K M,N$ and there are
$\K$-embeddings $f:M\to P$ and $g:N\to P$ with
$f\!\restriction A=g\!\restriction A$ and
$f(\ba)=g(\bb)$. Without amalgamation, we take
the transitive closure to obtain equivalence.
We abbreviate $\gtp(\ba;M):=\gtp(\ba/\emptyset;M)$
when the parameter base is empty; this is the
meaning of $\gtp(\ba;M)$ in the comparisons here.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Direct amalgamation versus strong maps at a categoricity size]
\label{lem:direct-amalgamation-reversal}
Suppose $\kappa\geq b_\K(\theta)$ is a categoricity
cardinal with $\kappa\geq\lambda_0+\theta$.
For $M,N\in\K_\kappa$ and tuples
$\ba\in M^\gamma$, $\bb\in N^\gamma$
of length $\gamma\leq\theta$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item There is a $\K$-embedding $f:M\to N$ with $f(\ba)=\bb$.
\item There are $\K$-embeddings $f_0:M\to P$ and
$f_1:N\to P$ with $f_0(\ba)=f_1(\bb)$.
\end{enumerate}
Here (ii) is \emph{direct amalgamation equivalence},
not the unrestricted transitive-closure definition of
$\gtp(-/\emptyset;-)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}\itshape 
The first condition implies the second by using
$P=N$, $f_0=f$, and $f_1=\operatorname{id}_N$.
For the converse, downward L\"owenheim--Skolem
and coherence give $P_0\leq_\K P$ of size $\kappa$
containing $f_0[M]\cup f_1[N]$, through which
both embeddings factor strongly. By categoricity,
choose $h:P_0\cong N$. Then $h f_1:N\to N$
is a $\K$-embedding. Apply eventual reversal to
$h f_1$ and $\bb$, obtaining $g:N\to N$ strong
with $g h f_1(\bb)=\bb$. Therefore
$g h f_0:M\to N$ carries $\ba$ to $\bb$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Arrow types and tail-restricted Galois equivalence]
\label{prop:arrow-galois}
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:direct-amalgamation-reversal},
let $\approx_{\geq\kappa}$ be the equivalence relation
on pairs $(\ba;M)$ with $M\in\K_{\geq\kappa}$
generated by direct amalgamation identifications
\emph{using only ambient and intermediate models
of size at least $\kappa$}.
For $M,N\in\K_{\geq\kappa}$ and tuples of
the same length $\gamma\leq\theta$,
\[
\atp_\kappa(\ba;M)=\atp_\kappa(\bb;N)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(\ba;M)\approx_{\geq\kappa}(\bb;N).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\atp_\kappa(\ba;M)=\atp_\kappa(\bb;N)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\gtp(\ba/\emptyset;M)=\gtp(\bb/\emptyset;N).
\]
The converse to the last implication is not
asserted without further hypotheses.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}\itshape 
If the arrow types agree, choose $\kappa$-sized
strong submodels $M_0\leq_\K M$ and
$N_0\leq_\K N$ containing the tuples,
and a strong embedding $f:M_0\to N_0$
carrying $\ba$ to $\bb$. The inclusions
and $f$ give a three-step chain of direct
identifications through $M_0,N_0$, all
of size at least $\kappa$.

Conversely, consider one direct identification
between $(\ba;M)$ and $(\bb;N)$ at sizes
at least $\kappa$, witnessed in $P$.
Choose $\kappa$-sized strong submodels
$M_0\leq_\K M$ and $N_0\leq_\K N$
containing the tuples. Their images in $P$
lie in a $\kappa$-sized strong submodel
$P_0\leq_\K P$ by downward
L\"owenheim--Skolem and coherence.
Lemma~\ref{lem:direct-amalgamation-reversal}
yields a strong map $M_0\to N_0$
identifying the tuples, hence equal
$\kappa$-arrow types. Transitivity of
arrow-type equality handles a finite
chain of direct identifications.
The implication to ordinary Galois types
follows because every allowed
$\approx_{\geq\kappa}$ comparison is also
allowed in the unrestricted relation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Earlier stabilization if amalgamation is assumed]
\label{cor:ap-lowers-stabilization}
If $\K$ has the amalgamation property, then at every
categoricity cardinal $\kappa\geq b_\K(\theta)$
with $\kappa\geq\lambda_0+\theta$,
\[
\atp_\kappa(\ba;M)=\atp_\kappa(\bb;N)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\gtp(\ba/\emptyset;M)=\gtp(\bb/\emptyset;N)
\]
for models $M,N$ of size at least $\kappa$ and
tuples of length at most $\theta$.
Hence the comparison projections between any two
such categoricity cardinals are already bijective.
This conditional conclusion cannot be used in the
manuscript before amalgamation has been established.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}\itshape 
Amalgamation makes direct amalgamation equivalence
transitive, so unrestricted Galois-type equality
has a direct witness. Restricting that witness to
$\kappa$-sized strong submodels as in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:arrow-galois} gives equal
arrow types. The converse is the same proposition.
For $\lambda\geq\kappa$ as in the statement,
both arrow comparisons compute the same unrestricted
Galois type, proving injectivity of the projection.
Surjectivity follows from the order-presented
extensions supplied by the EM functor.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the reversal threshold is not a stabilization threshold]
\label{rem:gtp-not-stabilization}
Ordinary Galois equivalence over $\emptyset$,
as defined above without amalgamation, allows
the intermediate models in its transitive
closure to have \emph{any} cardinality.
It can therefore be strictly coarser than
$\approx_{\geq\kappa}$, even for $\kappa$-sized
endpoints. The final sentence of the
previous version of Lemma~\ref{lem:direct-amalgamation-reversal}
incorrectly inferred equality for the
unrestricted transitive closure.

Neither \emph{no maximal models} nor the
L\"owenheim--Skolem property repairs this gap.
Consider two direct identifications
\[
M\longrightarrow P\longleftarrow A,
\qquad
A\longrightarrow Q\longleftarrow N
\]
through a small intermediate model $A$.
No maximal models lets us enlarge all three
witness models, and L\"owenheim--Skolem lets
us extract submodels of a prescribed sufficiently
large size. But these principles do not produce
\emph{one extension of $A$} which embeds
compatibly into both $P$ and $Q$.
That is an amalgamation requirement.
The star example below has no maximal models
and joint embedding, yet exhibits precisely
this obstruction.

As the cutoff $\kappa$ increases, the relations
$\approx_{\geq\kappa}$ can refine one another;
Proposition~4.2 of the source proves that
the associated arrow classes eventually stabilize
by a uniform counting argument. Invariance of
unrestricted Galois types under strong embeddings
does not by itself lower the stabilization threshold
to $b_\K(\theta)$. If $\K$ has amalgamation,
direct amalgamation equivalence is transitive
and the distinction disappears.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[Stars: a counterexample to unrestricted equivalence]
\label{ex:stars-gtp-arrow}
Let $\K$ have a binary relation $R$.
Its models are singletons and stars with a
distinguished center $c$ satisfying
$R(c,x)$ for every leaf $x$, and no other
instances of $R$. Declare every singleton
substructure strong, while a nonsingleton
substructure is strong precisely when it
contains the center of its ambient star.
This is an AEC with $\LS(\K)=\aleph_0$,
categorical in every infinite cardinal
(a model of size $\kappa$ is a star with
$\kappa$ leaves). It admits the usual
EM presentation with one constant for
the center and order-indexed leaves.

In a $\kappa$-sized star $M$, take its
center $c$ and a leaf $d$. Strong
self-embeddings preserve the center, so
\[
\atp_\kappa(c;M)\ne\atp_\kappa(d;M).
\]
Yet the unique element $x$ of a singleton
strong model $X$ can be embedded into $M$
as either $c$ or $d$. Hence
$(c;M)$ is directly equivalent to $(x;X)$
and $(x;X)$ directly equivalent to $(d;M)$,
giving
\[
\gtp(c/\emptyset;M)=\gtp(d/\emptyset;M).
\]
This AEC has \emph{no maximal models}: every
singleton extends strongly to arbitrarily large stars,
and every star can be extended by adding more leaves.
It also has joint embedding, since two stars can
be embedded into a larger star with their centers
identified. Neither property prevents the
singleton $X$ from serving as an intermediate
model in a Galois-equivalence chain that joins
distinct arrow types.

The reversal conclusion still holds for
tuples shorter than $\kappa$: a partial
inverse of a strong self-embedding on
fewer than $\kappa$ leaves extends to
an injection on all the leaves.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Colored arrows: arbitrarily long Galois zigzags]
\label{ex:colored-arrows-zigzags}
Consider the language with unary $T$ and binary
relations $(R_i)_{i<\omega}$. Think of $T(x)$ as
saying that $x$ is terminal, and of $R_i(x,y)$
as an outgoing edge of color $i$. Let
$\K_{\mathrm{col}}$ consist of structures satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item a vertex satisfying $T$ has no
outgoing $R_i$-edge;
\item every vertex not satisfying $T$
has at least one outgoing edge;
\item whenever $R_i(x,y)$ and $R_j(x,z)$
hold, $|i-j|\leq 1$.
\end{enumerate}
Take the strong-submodel relation to be
induced substructure inclusion between models
of $\K_{\mathrm{col}}$. Directed unions
preserve these conditions, and the
L\"owenheim--Skolem number is $\aleph_0$:
from any set of elements, iteratively add
witnesses for outgoing edges of nonterminal
vertices, in countably many steps.

Fix a terminal singleton $M=\{m\}$. For each
$i<\omega$, take a three-element model
$N_i=\{m,a_i,b_i\}$ with $m,b_i$ terminal
and $R_i(a_i,b_i)$ the unique edge. Write
$p_i=\gtp(a_i/M;N_i)$, using unrestricted
Galois equivalence as in our definitions.
More precisely, denote by
$q_i=(a_i,M,N_i)$ the \emph{pointed triple}
before taking its equivalence class.

The triples $q_i,q_{i+1}$ are directly
equivalent: identify $a_i$ with $a_{i+1}$
in a common model over $M$, retaining
their outgoing edges of adjacent colors.
But $q_i$ and $q_j$ are not directly
equivalent when $|i-j|\geq 2$: the
identified vertex would have outgoing
edges of colors differing by at least two.
Thus all the $q_i$ have the same \emph{Galois
type}, since direct equivalence is connected
by a finite chain, even though a direct
amalgam of the original endpoints need
not exist.

In fact, the shortest chain of direct
equivalences from $q_0$ to $q_n$
has length $n$. For a pointed triple
with nonterminal distinguished element,
let $m(q)$ be the minimum color of
an outgoing edge at that element.
A direct equivalence implies
$|m(q)-m(q')|\leq 1$, since both colors
must occur at the identified vertex
of an amalgam. Therefore a chain from
$m(q_0)=0$ to $m(q_n)=n$ requires
at least $n$ steps; the adjacent-color
chain attains this bound.

\emph{Comparison with stars.} The star
example shows that allowing \emph{small}
intermediate models can identify two
different arrow types, even with no
maximal models. This colored-arrow example
illustrates a separate phenomenon:
the transitive closure in the definition
of Galois type can require arbitrarily
long finite chains of direct amalgamations.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the characterization]
For ambient $M,N$ of size at least $\kappa$,
equality of $\kappa$-arrow types is equivalent
to equality in the \emph{tail-restricted}
equivalence $\approx_{\geq\kappa}$, not
necessarily to equality of unrestricted
Galois types. The latter always follows
from arrow-type equality but may hold without it,
as Example~\ref{ex:stars-gtp-arrow} shows.
This is an issue even when $M,N$ themselves
have cardinality $\kappa$: a global Galois-type
chain may pass through smaller models.

We write $S^\gamma_\kappa$ for the arrow-type
classes and $S^\gamma_*$ for their eventual
common value under the projections of
Proposition~4.2, understood up to canonical
bijections. These classes need not
be all global Galois types.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[EM-lifted Galois comparison]
Fix the EM template $\Phi$. A \emph{direct EM-lifted
comparison} between tuples $(\ba;\EM(I,\Phi))$ and
$(\bb;\EM(J,\Phi))$ consists of presentation records
$r=(\bar f,\bi)$ of $\ba$ and
$s=(\bar f,\bj)$ of $\bb$ with the same term sequence
and
\[
\operatorname{tp}(\bi;I)=\operatorname{tp}(\bj;J).
\]
Equivalently, the two index tuples have the same
Galois type over the empty set in $\Kord$:
there is a joint order extension identifying them.
The lifted order embeddings yield strong embeddings
of EM models identifying the evaluated tuples.

Write
\[
\gtp^{\mathrm{EM}}(\ba;\EM(I,\Phi))
=\gtp^{\mathrm{EM}}(\bb;\EM(J,\Phi))
\]
if the two tuples are equivalent under the
\emph{equivalence relation generated by direct
EM-lifted comparisons}. This is an invariant
of \emph{EM-presented tuples}, rather than the
usual AEC Galois type on all $\K$-models.
The use of transitive closure ensures that the
notation really denotes an equivalence class
even when a tuple has several presentation records.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Lifting quantifier-free order types]
\label{prop:order-gtpem}
Suppose $\ba$ and $\bb$ admit records
$(\bar f,\bi)$ and $(\bar f,\bj)$ with
$\operatorname{tp}(\bi;I)=\operatorname{tp}(\bj;J)$.
Then
\[
\gtp^{\mathrm{EM}}(\ba;\EM(I,\Phi))
=\gtp^{\mathrm{EM}}(\bb;\EM(J,\Phi)),
\]
and their natural diagrams coincide.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}\itshape 
Amalgamate $I$ and $J$ over the labeled support
isomorphism $\bi\mapsto\bj$, obtaining order
embeddings into a common linear order $L$.
By EM functoriality, the induced $\K$-embeddings
of the reducts into $\EM(L,\Phi)$ identify
the tuples. This is a direct EM-lifted comparison.
Extending $L$ to a sufficiently large
categoricity-order presentation and using
preservation of stabilized arrow types proves
equality of natural diagrams.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The comparison under investigation]
The notation $\gtp^{\mathrm{EM}}$ in these notes
is a relation on \emph{tuples in EM models}, generated
by comparisons induced from order embeddings.
The natural diagram is also ultimately an invariant
of the evaluated tuple, although a record is used
to compute it. The genuine difference is the
\emph{class of morphisms permitted}: order-induced
EM embeddings for $\gtp^{\mathrm{EM}}$, but
arbitrary strong maps in the stabilized arrow type.
The former implies equality of natural diagrams.
Example~\ref{ex:pure-sets-order} below shows that
the converse can fail. We retain $\gtp^{\mathrm{EM}}$
primarily to exhibit the distinction between the
order-induced and the full AEC comparisons;
it is a useful auxiliary invariant, but is not
expected to play a central role in the subsequent
arguments. The working calculus is based on
$\atp$ and its EM-based extension $\natatp$.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{Natural and placed canonical diagrams}
\begin{definition}[Natural arrow type (source: natural diagram); {\cite[Definition 4.4]{oai-ecc}}]
\label{def:natural-diagram}
Fix a linear order $I$, an output ordinal $\gamma$,
and a presentation record
$r=(\bar f,\bi)\in\Rec_\tau^\gamma(I,\Phi)$.
Choose an order extension $j:I\hookrightarrow I'$
whose cardinality is a sufficiently large
categoricity cardinal for the stabilized arrow
type of a $\gamma$-tuple. Write
$j_*r=(\bar f,j(\bi))$ for the induced record.
Define
\[
\natatp(I,r)
:=
\atp\bigl(\operatorname{ev}_{I'}^\gamma(j_*r);
          \EM(I',\Phi)\bigr).
\]
This is the \emph{natural diagram computed from
$(I,r)$}. The purpose of $I'$ is to evaluate the
record at a sufficiently large categoricity cardinal
where $\atp$ is defined and stabilized, not to
introduce a new type invariant. Neither its choice
nor the chosen record affects the result, as proved below.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Natural diagrams descend from records to tuples]
\label{prop:nat-record-independent}
Let $I$ be a linear order and
$r,s\in\Rec_\tau^\gamma(I,\Phi)$ be records with
\[
\operatorname{ev}_I^\gamma(r)
=\operatorname{ev}_I^\gamma(s)=\ba.
\]
Then
$\natatp(I,r)=\natatp(I,s)$.
Furthermore, the result of Definition~\ref{def:natural-diagram}
is independent of the sufficiently large extension $I'$.
Thus it is well-defined to write
\[
\natatp(\ba;\EM(I,\Phi))
:=\natatp(I,r)
\]
for \emph{any} presentation record $r$ of $\ba$.
If $|\EM(I,\Phi)|$ is already above the relevant
stabilization threshold, this equals
$\atp(\ba;\EM(I,\Phi))$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}\itshape 
For a common order extension $j:I\hookrightarrow I'$,
functoriality of the expanded EM presentation gives
\[
\operatorname{ev}_{I'}^\gamma(j_*r)
=\operatorname{EM}_{\tau}(j,\Phi)(\ba)
=\operatorname{ev}_{I'}^\gamma(j_*s).
\]
They therefore have the same stabilized arrow type.
For two different sufficiently large extensions
$I'_0,I'_1$, amalgamate the linear orders over
the common copy of $I$, and extend the resulting
order if necessary to another sufficiently large
categoricity cardinal. The induced strong EM maps
identify the evaluated tuple, so preservation of
stabilized arrow types makes the two values equal.
Finally, if the original reduct is already large
enough to carry $\atp$, its strong embedding into
any such extension preserves $\atp(\ba;-)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Ambient invariance and the role of the EM presentation]
\label{rem:nat-ambient}
For any fixed large-enough categoricity cardinal $\kappa$,
$\atp_\kappa(\ba;M)$ is already preserved by every
strong embedding $M\to N$ of models of size at
least $\kappa$; it does \emph{not} depend on which
strong extension of such an $M$ is used.
The potential difficulties are instead:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item the arrow comparisons may refine as $\kappa$ increases,
until their eventual stabilization; and
\item a small model $M$ may admit \emph{incompatible}
strong extensions whose images of $\ba$ have different
stabilized arrow types. No-maximal-models alone does
not provide a well-defined choice of type.
\end{enumerate}
The special advantage of an EM presentation
$\EM(I,\Phi)$ is that \emph{order extensions}
$I\hookrightarrow I'$ provide a compatible directed
family of strong extensions. Since linear orders
amalgamate over a common suborder, the stabilized
arrow type calculated in this family is independent
of the chosen sufficiently large $I'$.
There is no distinguished canonical order $I'$,
and the extension itself is neither canonical nor
natural in a categorical sense. Instead, the
\emph{value of the type} is independent of every
permitted choice of $I'$, relative to the fixed
template and EM presentation. In this precise
sense $\natatp$ gives a canonical extension of
the \emph{stabilized arrow-type invariant} to
tuples in smaller EM-presented models, rather
than a canonical extension of the models themselves.
For models already above the stabilization
threshold, it coincides with the intrinsic $\atp$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Order-pattern sufficiency for natural diagrams]
\label{prop:order-pattern-nat}
Suppose records $r=(\bar f,\bi)$ over $I$ and
$s=(\bar f,\bj)$ over $J$ have the same term list
and satisfy
$\operatorname{tp}(\bi;I)=\operatorname{tp}(\bj;J)$.
Then
\[
\natatp(I,r)=\natatp(J,s).
\]
No occupancy information about the ambient orders
is required. This is a \emph{sufficient} criterion,
not a characterization of diagram equality.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}\itshape 
The supports are isomorphic as ordered lists.
Amalgamate $I$ and $J$ over that support isomorphism
and lift the order embeddings through the EM functor.
The resulting strong embeddings identify
the evaluated tuples; extend to a sufficiently
large categoricity order and use preservation
of stabilized arrow types. Equivalently,
apply Proposition~\ref{prop:order-gtpem}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[EM-lifted comparison implies natural-diagram equality]
\label{cor:gtpem-to-nat}
For tuples $\ba,\bb$ in EM-presented models,
\[
\gtp^{\mathrm{EM}}(\ba)=\gtp^{\mathrm{EM}}(\bb)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\natatp(\ba)=\natatp(\bb),
\]
where the right side abbreviates the record-independent
tuple notation of Proposition~\ref{prop:nat-record-independent}.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}\itshape 
Each generating direct EM-lifted comparison is induced
by common records and the same quantifier-free order
type, so Proposition~\ref{prop:order-pattern-nat}
gives equality of natural diagrams. This equality
persists along a finite chain of generating comparisons.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[The converse fails even for pure sets]
\label{ex:pure-sets-order}
Let $\K$ be the AEC of pure sets with strong
embeddings the injections, categorical in
every infinite cardinal. Take an EM template
whose expanded structure $\operatorname{EM}_{\tau}(I,\Phi)$
has underlying set $I$, remembers its linear
order $<$, and has no functions other than
variables as terms. Its reduct is the pure set $I$.

Choose $i<j$ in $I$ and let
$\ba=(i,j)$ and $\bb=(j,i)$.
In the pure-set reduct, an automorphism
interchanges $i$ and $j$, hence
\[
\natatp(\ba;\EM(I,\Phi))
=\natatp(\bb;\EM(I,\Phi)).
\]
But every order-induced embedding preserves
whether the first tuple coordinate precedes
the second in the skeleton. This property
is preserved under finite chains of
EM-lifted comparisons, so
\[
\gtp^{\mathrm{EM}}(\ba;\EM(I,\Phi))
\ne
\gtp^{\mathrm{EM}}(\bb;\EM(I,\Phi)).
\]
One can present $\ba$ using the term sequence
$(x_0,x_1)$ and $\bb$ using $(x_1,x_0)$
over the same increasing support $(i,j)$;
there are no alternative term presentations
that remove the reversal in this template.
Thus even the \emph{existence} of records
with equal natural diagrams does not imply
equality of the EM-lifted type.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Resolution of the earlier review question]
The tuple-invariant interpretation of
$\natatp$ is legitimate by
Proposition~\ref{prop:nat-record-independent}.
The distinction from $\gtp^{\mathrm{EM}}$
is not dependence on a chosen record:
both assign equivalence classes to tuples in their
respective EM presentations. However,
$\gtp^{\mathrm{EM}}$ remembers the chosen order
presentation in its permitted witnesses, whereas
$\natatp$ is intrinsic to the evaluated
AEC tuple once sufficiently large.
The EM-lifted relation uses a more restrictive
family of witnesses. Example~\ref{ex:pure-sets-order}
shows that equality of natural diagrams is
strictly weaker in general. Accordingly we
retain two distinct names rather than define
$\gtp^{\mathrm{EM}}:=\natatp$.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Placement and placed diagram over a base;
{\cite[Definition 4.4]{oai-ecc}}]
Fix $A=\EM(I,\Phi)$, an enumeration $\ba_A$ of its elements,
and term representations of that enumeration using positions
in $I$. A \emph{placement} of an additional tuple $\bd$ over $A$
is a labeled term representation of $\bd$ in an order extension
$I\subseteq J$, retaining the fixed positions and term
representations of all parameters in $\ba_A$. The
\emph{placed comparison diagram} of this placement is
\[
D_{\mathrm{pl}}(\bd;\,A,\text{placement})
:=\natatp((\bd,\ba_A);\EM(J,\Phi)),
\]
where the right side uses the combined labeled term representation.
It is computed in a sufficiently large EM extension, which need
not have the cardinality of $A$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Relative order records and cuts]
A placement's order-theoretic data may be described by the
quantifier-free order type of the new support coordinates over
the fixed skeleton $I$, together with the term labels and the
information specifying which positions occur in each term.
Equivalently, record the cuts in $I$ of the new coordinates and
their mutual order/equality relations (especially for coordinates
in the same cut). These are cuts of the \emph{skeleton order},
not necessarily cuts of the underlying structure $A$.

Thus a placement resembles a presentation record with parameters,
but its \emph{placed diagram} is a \emph{natural diagram with
parameters}, not an EM-automorphism orbit presentation record.
Matching relative labeled order records implies matching placed
diagrams; the converse is not asserted. Nor do we identify
placed diagrams with Galois types over arbitrary bases before
the relevant amalgamation results have been proved.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to types over models]
For $A=\EM(I,\Phi)$ and a tuple $\bd$ in an EM extension,
the placed comparison is
$\natatp((\bd,\ba_A);\EM(J,\Phi))$, with the enumeration
of $A$ fixed on both sides. This resembles the usual
AEC notion $\operatorname{gtp}(\bd/A;N)$, but the
definition requires neither a monster model nor
amalgamation over $A$. Matching the labeled relative
support order pattern is sufficient for equality of
placed diagrams, not necessary. Conversely, equality
of placed diagrams need not imply that the two chosen
presentations have isomorphic labeled support orders.
\end{remark}


\begin{remark}[Comparisons between the type notions]
For tuples represented by a common term list $\bar f$,
the implications proved here can be summarized as
\[
\tpstar(\bi;H(\delta))=\tpstar(\bj;H(\delta))
\Longrightarrow
\EMauttp(\ba;M_\delta)=\EMauttp(\bb;M_\delta)
\Longrightarrow
\auttp(\ba;M_\delta)=\auttp(\bb;M_\delta).
\]
The first implication assumes the usual bound
$\ell(\bi)<\operatorname{cf}(\delta)$.
For possibly distinct index orders we have
\[
\operatorname{tp}(\bi;I)=\operatorname{tp}(\bj;J)
\Longrightarrow
\gtp^{\mathrm{EM}}(\ba;\EM(I,\Phi))
=\gtp^{\mathrm{EM}}(\bb;\EM(J,\Phi))
\Longrightarrow
\natatp(\ba;\EM(I,\Phi))
=\natatp(\bb;\EM(J,\Phi)).
\]
These implications do not assert converses.

At a sufficiently large categoricity cardinal,
$\atp_\kappa$ is exactly tail-restricted Galois
equivalence by Proposition~\ref{prop:arrow-galois}.
It implies ordinary Galois-type equality over
$\emptyset$, but the converse can fail without
amalgamation, even at cardinality $\kappa$.
Similarly, a placed diagram
over $A=\EM(I,\Phi)$ is an arrow comparison with an
enumeration of $A$ included as parameters; it is not
declared to equal a Galois type over arbitrary $A$.
\end{remark}

\section{Consequences: order symmetry, locality, and amalgamation}
\subsection{Back-and-forth and block transpositions}
\begin{remark}[Source Proposition 4.3: back-and-forth]
At a closure point $\zeta$ of countable cofinality, write both
$\zeta$-models as countable increasing unions of bounded sets.
Alternately extend tuples with matching diagrams, using source
Proposition~4.2 to obtain strong maps between bounded strong
submodels. The union of the resulting partial correspondences
is an isomorphism because atomic information is preserved.
The argument does not require compatible EM resolutions of
the two models. At uncountable cofinality there are intermediate
limit stages, where preservation of a possibly infinite matched
tuple's diagram needs additional justification.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Disjoint block transpositions;
{\cite[Lemma 4.6]{oai-ecc}}]
Fix a linear order $I$ and a labeled EM presentation
$M=\EM(I,\Phi)$. Let $\bd,\be$ be (possibly set-sized)
tuples of elements of $M$, and choose term presentations
\[
d_\alpha=t_\alpha(\bar i_\alpha),\qquad
e_\beta=u_\beta(\bar j_\beta)
\]
in the skeleton of $I$. Let $S_d,S_e\subseteq I$ be the
unions of the skeleton supports of these presentations,
and put $S=S_d\cup S_e$.

Suppose $(C_j)_{j\in J}$ is a family of pairwise disjoint
convex subsets of the \emph{joint support order} $S$,
indexed in increasing order: for $j<j'$ every point of
$C_j$ precedes every point of $C_{j'}$. Write
\[
X_j=C_j\cap S_d,\qquad Y_j=C_j\cap S_e.
\]
Assume each region is the concatenation of two blocks,
either $X_j<Y_j$ or $Y_j<X_j$, and that the blocks are
\emph{exclusive}:
\[
X_j\cap S_e=\varnothing,\qquad
Y_j\cap S_d=\varnothing.
\]
In particular no skeleton coordinate used by both
families lies in a transposed region. No assumption
that $X_j$ and $Y_j$ are order-isomorphic is needed.

Construct a new ordered support $S'$ by replacing
$X_j+Y_j$ with $Y_j+X_j$ (or conversely) for each $j$,
retaining the internal order of every block and the
order of all other support positions. Regard the same
support coordinates as labeled positions in $S'$:
each occurrence of a coordinate in $t_\alpha$ or
$u_\beta$ retains its original assignment to that
coordinate. Thus the first family still uses the
$X_j$-coordinates and the second still uses the
$Y_j$-coordinates; \emph{only their relative order
changes}. Evaluate these same terms in an EM model
over an order $J$ containing $S'$, obtaining tuples
$\bd'$ and $\be'$. Then
\[
\natatp((\bd,\be);\EM(I,\Phi))
=
\natatp((\bd',\be');\EM(J,\Phi)),
\]
The new tuples need not live in the original $M$;
the statement compares their natural diagrams in
(possibly different) EM presentations.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]\itshape 
Suppose the original and transposed diagrams differ.
Choose $\sigma$ bounding the two term families and supports,
and $\chi$ sufficiently large with $\chi^\sigma=\chi$ for
the orbit bound in source Lemma~4.5. Repeat the exclusive
blocks along an auxiliary order $L$, with orientations
chosen so that the diagram of a first-family tuple $d_i$
with a second-family tuple $e_j$ is the original diagram
when $i<j$, and the transposed diagram when $i>j$.

Choose $L$ with at most $\chi$ divider positions and more
than $\chi$ branch positions realizing distinct cuts over
the dividers. Explicitly, let $\epsilon$ be the least infinite
cardinal with $2^\epsilon>\chi$ and use the lexicographically
ordered binary tree $2^{<\epsilon}$, with a divider between
each pair of successor subtrees and a point for each branch.
Fix the tuples $e_j$ at divider positions, together with
the shared support, as parameters (at most $\chi$ of them).
For distinct branch indices $i,i'$, a divider $j$ separates
them, and the two diagrams of $(d_i,e_j)$ and
$(d_{i'},e_j)$ differ. Hence the $d_i$ realize more than
$\chi$ automorphism orbits over these parameters.
Embed the construction into a sufficiently large
categoricity EM presentation, contradicting Lemma~4.5.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No-order-property interpretation of Lemma 4.6]
\emph{Interpretive remark, not a separately stated theorem of the source.}
If swapping exclusive blocks changed a joint natural diagram from
$p$ to $q\neq p$, the repetition construction in Lemma~4.6
would yield, for arbitrarily large linear orders $L$, tuples
$(\bd_i)_{i\in L}$ and $(\be_j)_{j\in L}$ satisfying
\[
D(\bd_i,\be_j)=
\begin{cases}
p,&i<j,\\
q,&i>j.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus a failure of block symmetry would witness an
\emph{order property for canonical diagrams}.
Lemma~4.5 rules this out by bounding automorphism orbits
over a small parameter set. The conceptual progression is
\[
\text{bounded automorphism orbits}
\Longrightarrow
\text{no such diagram order pattern}
\Longrightarrow
\text{disjoint-block symmetry}.
\]
This resembles the classical stability argument turning
order-indiscernibles into permutation-indiscernibles.
It does not say that all order information on an EM skeleton
is invisible in the reduct: only the specified transpositions
of disjoint exclusive support blocks are covered.

Ordinary first-order no-order-property is not automatically
sufficient: the term families and diagrams may have arbitrary
set-sized length, bounded by $\sigma$, and amalgamation
has not yet been established. A cardinal-parameterized
no-canonical-diagram-order-property principle might subsume
Lemma~4.6, but the exact formulation and its relation to
Lemma~4.5 remain to be checked.

\textbf{Problem for later.} Formulate a parameterized
\emph{no-order-property for natural arrow types}
which implies disjoint block transposition.
Compare it first to the first-order stable-theory
argument that indiscernible sequences are
indiscernible sets, and then ask whether an
appropriate stability or superstability
hypothesis for AECs with a monster model
implies that principle. The present proof
uses unbounded categoricity to obtain the
uniform orbit bounds, so it does not
immediately provide such a result.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Locality and compactness for placed comparisons}
\label{sec:locality}
\paragraph{Natural arrow types over parameter sets.}
Fix a well-ordered EM presentation
$A=\EM(I,\Phi)$ and a tuple-length bound $\sigma$.
For any set $X\subseteq A$, let
\[
S^\sigma_{\natatp}(X;A)
:=
\bigl\{\natatp((\bd,\bar x);\EM(J,\Phi)):
 I\hookrightarrow J,\ \bd\in\EM(J,\Phi)^\sigma\bigr\},
\]
where $\bar x$ is an enumeration of $X$,
transported along the order-induced embedding.
The comparison identifies the parameter coordinates
according to their fixed names; its equality does
not depend on the choice of enumeration.
For $Y\subseteq X$, restriction of the parameter list
gives a well-defined map
\[
\restriction Y:
S^\sigma_{\natatp}(X;A)\longrightarrow
S^\sigma_{\natatp}(Y;A).
\]
We suppress the ambient presentation $A$ when it
is fixed, writing $S^\sigma_{\natatp}(X)$.
The same construction works for \emph{any subset}
$X$ of an EM base, whether or not $X$ is the
universe of a strong submodel. It does not
automatically define a natural arrow type
over an arbitrary non-EM base.

The source's Proposition~4.8 gives a definable
nondecreasing cardinal function $g(\sigma)$
with the following \emph{separation property}:
two distinct placed natural arrow types over
$A$ are distinguished by their restrictions
to some $X\subseteq A$ with $|X|\leq g(\sigma)$.
This is tameness-like locality for
the \emph{placed comparison} invariant,
not a general tameness statement for
ordinary Galois types.

\begin{lemma}[Unique compactness for natural arrow types over an EM base;
consequence of {\cite[Propositions 4.8--4.9]{oai-ecc}}]
\label{lem:unique-placed-compactness}
Let $I$ be an infinite well-order,
$A=\EM(I,\Phi)$, and let $\sigma$ be infinite.
Choose an infinite cardinal $\chi$ satisfying
\[
\chi>\lambda_0+\sigma,\qquad
\chi^\sigma=\chi,\qquad
\chi\geq g(\sigma).
\]
For each $X\in[A]^{\leq\chi}$, suppose we
have specified a \emph{possible} type
\[
p_X\in S^\sigma_{\natatp}(X;A)
\]
such that
\[
X\subseteq Y,\quad |Y|\leq\chi
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
p_Y\restriction X=p_X.
\]
Then there exists exactly one
\[
p\in S^\sigma_{\natatp}(A;A)
\]
satisfying $p\restriction X=p_X$
for all $X\in[A]^{\leq\chi}$.
Uniqueness is of the \emph{type}, not of
a realizing tuple or an order extension.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}\itshape
Choose an enumeration for every parameter set,
with the identifications made explicit when
taking restrictions. Each $p_X$ is, by definition,
a possible joint canonical diagram of an added
tuple with the parameters $X$. Its parameter
restriction is the natural diagram of $X$,
and the assumed coherence is exactly the
compatibility hypothesis of source
Proposition~4.9. That proposition produces
a placement of $\bd$ over $I$ realizing all
the prescribed diagrams, hence a type
$p\in S^\sigma_{\natatp}(A;A)$ with
$p\restriction X=p_X$.

If $p,p'$ are two such types, source
Proposition~4.8 finds, whenever $p\ne p'$,
a subset $X\subseteq A$ of size at most
$g(\sigma)\leq\chi$ on which they differ.
Both restrictions equal $p_X$,
contradicting the choice of $X$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The quantifier over parameter subsets]
The statement concerns $X\in[A]^{\leq\chi}$.
Asking for $p_X$ for \emph{all} subsets
$X\subseteq A$, including $X=A$, would
already supply the desired conclusion
as $p_A$. If $|A|\leq\chi$, the theorem
is correspondingly tautological; its
interest is when $|A|>\chi$.
The hypothesis that each $p_X$ is
\emph{possible} is essential.

If $A_0=\EM(I_0,\Phi)\leq_\K A$ for
an infinite sub-well-order $I_0\subseteq I$,
the same lemma applies directly with
$A_0$ as the base. This covers EM submodels.
For an arbitrary subset $Y\subseteq A$,
the notation $S^\sigma_{\natatp}(Y;A)$ is
still meaningful, but the stated theorem
is over the \emph{whole EM base} $A$.
A corresponding compactness theorem based
only on prescriptions on $[Y]^{\leq\chi}$
would require adapting the support-profile
argument of source Proposition~4.9
and the uniform testing bound of
Proposition~4.8 to parameters restricted
to $Y$. We do not assert that extension
here. Likewise, arbitrary strong submodels
of $A$ need not be EM submodels with the
induced presentation.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Gluing types along a $\chi$-covering chain]
\label{prop:covering-chain-gluing}
Keep the hypotheses on $A,\sigma,\chi$
from Lemma~\ref{lem:unique-placed-compactness}.
Suppose $A=\bigcup_{i<\delta}A_i$ for an
increasing family of parameter subsets
(not necessarily submodels), and
each $X\in[A]^{\leq\chi}$ is contained
in some $A_i$.
If
\[
p_i\in S^\sigma_{\natatp}(A_i;A)
\qquad(i<\delta)
\]
satisfy $p_j\restriction A_i=p_i$
whenever $i<j$, then there is a unique
$p\in S^\sigma_{\natatp}(A;A)$
extending all the $p_i$.
In particular, the covering assumption holds
whenever $\operatorname{cf}(\delta)>\chi$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}\itshape
For each $X\in[A]^{\leq\chi}$, choose $i$
with $X\subseteq A_i$ and put
$p_X=p_i\restriction X$.
This is independent of the chosen $i$:
any two indices have a common later index,
and the chain is coherent. The $p_X$
are possible and satisfy the compatibility
conditions of Lemma~\ref{lem:unique-placed-compactness},
which gives existence and uniqueness of $p$.
For the last assertion, if $|X|\leq\chi$,
choose for each $x\in X$ an index $i_x$
with $x\in A_{i_x}$. Since
$\operatorname{cf}(\delta)>\chi$,
the supremum of those indices is below
$\delta$, so $X$ lies in some $A_i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The countable-union problem remains open here]
\label{rem:countable-chain-natural-types}
Suppose $A=\bigcup_{n<\omega}A_n$ and
$p_n\in S^\sigma_{\natatp}(A_n;A)$
are coherent under restriction.
The assumption in
Proposition~\ref{prop:covering-chain-gluing}
usually fails: one can choose elements
from cofinally many successive stages to
obtain a countable set $X\subseteq A$
which is contained in no $A_n$.
Since $\chi$ is infinite, the $p_n$
alone do not assign a diagram to this $X$.
Thus Lemma~\ref{lem:unique-placed-compactness}
does not prove that the $p_n$ have a unique
common extension over $A$.

One might seek a stronger \emph{countable-chain
continuity} principle for placed natural
arrow types. Its existence and uniqueness
parts are distinct questions: even if a
global extension exists, the uniform
testing bound $g(\sigma)$ only separates
different global types over a parameter
set of size at most $g(\sigma)$, which
need not lie in one $A_n$.
For comparison, the source's Lemma~4.10
handles \emph{bounded diagrams of an actual
tuple in an ambient strong extension} and
uses additional closed-cardinal hypotheses;
it does not state arbitrary countable-chain
continuity for abstract type prescriptions.

If $p_n$ are instead all types \emph{over
the same base $A$}, there may be a different
issue: their \emph{tuple lengths} could be
growing. This is distinct from the chain
of parameter sets considered here; see
the next remark. If the base and tuple
length are both fixed, an increasing
sequence in the sense of restriction is
simply a constant type.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Countable unions in the tuple coordinates]
\label{rem:long-tuple-union}
Fix the same EM base $A$ and ordinals
$\gamma_n$ with $\gamma_n\leq\gamma_{n+1}$,
$\gamma=\sup_{n<\omega}\gamma_n$.
Suppose
\[
p_n\in S^{\gamma_n}_{\natatp}(A;A),
\qquad
p_{n+1}\restriction\gamma_n=p_n,
\]
where the restriction now discards \emph{tuple
coordinates}, not parameters. Does there
exist a unique
$p\in S^\gamma_{\natatp}(A;A)$
with $p\restriction\gamma_n=p_n$?

This is a different continuity question.
Our compact-placement Lemma~\ref{lem:unique-placed-compactness}
works at a \emph{fixed} upper bound on
the entire added tuple length. To use it
here, one would first need, for every
$X\in[A]^{\leq\chi}$, a \emph{possible}
joint diagram $q_X$ for the entire
$\gamma$-tuple with parameters $X$,
whose restrictions agree with
$p_n\restriction X$ for all $n$ and
whose parameter restrictions are coherent.
If such $q_X$ are supplied and
$\gamma\leq\sigma$ with the stated
conditions on $\chi$, the lemma gives
a unique resulting \emph{full} type.
But existence of these $q_X$ is not
a consequence proved here of the
coherence of the $p_n$ alone.

Even assuming existence, to show uniqueness
\emph{from the $p_n$ alone} one would need
a continuity principle saying that
two natural arrow types of a long tuple
which agree on every initial
$\gamma_n$-subtuple are equal.
The uniform testing theorem is about
\emph{parameter subsets} of bounded size,
not continuity in the number of
distinguished tuple coordinates.
This is precisely the distinction between
a \emph{tameness-like} principle (small
parameter sets detect unequal types)
and a \emph{type-shortness-like} principle
(small coordinate subtuples detect
unequal long types). The source proves
the former for placed comparisons, but
the latter is not a stated consequence.

The countable exhaustion in the proof of
source Theorem~4.11 uses \emph{actual
compatible enumerations} of strong
submodels and their already determined
bounded diagrams. Its construction
extends realizations stage by stage.
It does not assert this unrestricted
inverse-limit property for arbitrary
abstract types of growing tuple length.
This distinction is worth revisiting
when auditing that construction.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Scope of compactness and uniqueness]
This is a compactness-and-separation principle for
\emph{placed canonical diagrams over well-ordered
EM presentations}, not a compactness theorem for
arbitrary Galois types. The possibility hypothesis
on each $q_X$ is essential; coherence alone does
not assert that an arbitrary syntactic prescription
is a possible joint diagram. The bound $g(\sigma)$
is independent of $|I|$. In Lemma~4.10 the source
applies this principle at cofinally many
$\chi<\mu$ to lift all bounded diagrams of an
actual tuple over a base of size $\mu$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The role of cofinality in the next step]
The compactness and uniform-testing propositions are
formulated for arbitrary infinite well-ordered skeletons.
In the source, Lemma~4.10 applies them at strong-limit
cardinals $\mu$ of countable cofinality that are
strictly closed below themselves under the relevant
threshold functions $t$ and $g$. The proof of that
lemma uses cofinally many $\chi<\mu$ with
$\chi^\sigma=\chi$ (available from the strong-limit
assumption), whereas countable cofinality is central
to the later countable exhaustion used for
amalgamation in Theorem~4.11.
The weaker cofinality requirements that might suffice
for Lemma~4.10 alone have not been established as a
separate source theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Amalgamation at selected levels; {\cite[Theorem 4.11]{oai-ecc}}]\label{thm:amalgamation-closed}
There is an unbounded class of strong-limit cardinals $\mu$ of countable cofinality such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item $\K$ is categorical in $\mu$, and two $\mu$-sized strong extensions of a $\mu$-model admit a strong amalgam of size $\mu$.
\item For bounded tuples $\bar d_i\in B_i$ over a common $\mu$-sized base $A$, an amalgam identifying the tuples exists exactly when their relative diagrams agree on all $X\subseteq A$ of size at most $g(\sigma)$, for the relevant tuple-length bound $\sigma$.
\item There are at most $\mu$ singleton types over each $\mu$-model, where ``type'' is identification in an amalgam over the model.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proposition}[Placed natural arrow types are Galois types at closed levels]
\label{prop:closed-level-gtp-characterization}
Let $\mu$ be a cardinal to which source
Lemma~4.10 and Theorem~4.11 apply.
Suppose $A\leq_\K B_0,B_1$ are all of
cardinality $\mu$ and $\bd_i\in B_i^\gamma$
for the same $\gamma\leq\sigma<\mu$.
Fix an isomorphism $A\cong\EM(I,\Phi)$
for a well-order $I$ of size $\mu$.
Let $p_i\in S^\gamma_{\natatp}(A;A)$
be the \emph{unique placed lift} of the
bounded diagrams
\[
\atp((\bd_i,\bar x);B_i),
\qquad X\subseteq A,\quad
|X|<\mu,
\]
where $\bar x$ enumerates $X$,
and the arrow type is computed at a
stabilization level below $\mu$
appropriate to the tuple length.
Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $p_0=p_1$;
\item the bounded diagrams of
$(\bd_0,X;B_0)$ and
$(\bd_1,X;B_1)$ agree for every
$X\subseteq A$ with $|X|\leq g(\sigma)$;
\item there exists a strong amalgam
of $B_0,B_1$ over $A$ identifying
$\bd_0,\bd_1$;
\item $\gtp(\bd_0/A;B_0)=\gtp(\bd_1/A;B_1)$,
with Galois types defined by unrestricted
transitive closure over $A$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, at such levels the equality
relation on placed natural arrow types
of tuples in $\mu$-sized extensions
\emph{coincides with} equality of
ordinary Galois types over the $\mu$-base.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}\itshape
The existence and uniqueness of $p_i$
is source Lemma~4.10. Source Proposition~4.8
shows that two placed lifts agree if and
only if their restrictions to parameter
sets of size at most $g(\sigma)$ agree;
this gives (i)$\Leftrightarrow$(ii).
The equivalence of (ii) and (iii) is
exactly source Theorem~4.11(2).

An amalgam identifying the tuples immediately
gives equality of Galois types. Conversely,
a chain witnessing equality of Galois types
\emph{over the fixed base $A$} cannot pass
through models smaller than $\mu$, since
every intermediate model contains a strong
copy of $A$. Downward L\"owenheim--Skolem
and coherence shrink each direct comparison
to a comparison among $\mu$-sized strong
extensions of $A$ (and $\mu$-sized amalgams).
The amalgamation property for such models,
asserted in Theorem~4.11(1), makes direct
equivalence over $A$ transitive.
Consequently the entire finite chain
collapses to a single identifying
$\mu$-amalgam. This proves
(iv)$\Rightarrow$(iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Not simply a new name for Galois types]
\label{rem:arrow-novelty}
The preceding proposition provides a
\emph{Galois-type characterization after
amalgamation has been proved}, at the
special closed levels of Theorem~4.11.
Before that theorem, stabilized arrow
types and their placed lifts are available
without assuming amalgamation, and they
supply the comparison machinery used to
\emph{establish} amalgamation. This is
the distinctive role of the construction
in the manuscript; see also
Remark~\ref{rem:reversal-homogeneity}
on eventual reversal as a local substitute
for homogeneity. On arbitrary smaller
parameter subsets of $A$, the placed
invariant is defined, but it need not
already coincide with ordinary Galois
types over that subset.

Both eventual reversal (source Theorem~3.11)
and stabilization (source Proposition~4.2)
use unbounded categoricity essentially
in their stated proofs. In an AEC with
amalgamation and a monster model,
Galois types already admit an automorphism-orbit
description, whereas the directed strong-map
comparison between arbitrary models of size
$\kappa$ need not be symmetric without
additional universality or homogeneity.
It is an open question for these notes
whether an appropriate version of the
arrow-type machinery can be useful in
superstable AECs without the unbounded
categoricity hypothesis; no such general
theorem is being claimed here.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Scope of amalgamation]
This is amalgamation at selected cardinals, not an assertion
of eventual amalgamation throughout a tail. The first conclusion
is distinct from the uniform tameness and compactness of
placed diagrams established earlier.
\end{remark}

\section{Finite systems and working schemes}
\subsection{Systems and their morphisms}
We make the systems of \cite[Section 5.1]{oai-ecc} precise. Requiring
internal edge maps to be strong is our explicit convention;
the source initially says \emph{specified maps} without
separately requiring their strongness.

\begin{definition}[Model embeddings]
Let $\tau=\tau(\K)$. A $\tau$-embedding $f:M\to N$ is an
embedding of $\tau$-structures. A \emph{$\K$-embedding}, equivalently
a \emph{strong embedding}, is a $\tau$-embedding satisfying
$f[M]\leq_\K N$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Finite shape and system]\label{def:finite-system}
Fix a finite directed multigraph $G=(V,E,s,t)$, where $s,t:E\to V$.
A \emph{$G$-system} is
\[
\mathcal M=((M_v)_{v\in V},(f_e^\mathcal M)_{e\in E}),
\]
where each $M_v\in\K$ and each
$f_e^\mathcal M:M_{s(e)}\to M_{t(e)}$ is a $\K$-embedding.
The shape may carry distinguished named sorted lists.
No intersection-of-images or path-commutativity axiom is implicit.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[System embeddings and isomorphisms]
A \emph{$\tau$-embedding of systems} $h:\mathcal M\to\mathcal N$
is a family $h_v:M_v\to N_v$ of $\tau$-embeddings such that
\[
h_{t(e)}\circ f_e^\mathcal M=f_e^\mathcal N\circ h_{s(e)}
\quad(e\in E),
\]
preserving all distinguished named lists.
It is a \emph{$\K$-embedding of systems} if every
$h_v$ is a $\K$-embedding. An \emph{isomorphism of systems}
is an invertible system embedding, equivalently a commuting
family of component isomorphisms onto the whole target vertices.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Working-scheme arrows]
An \emph{arrow} is a designated $\K$-embedding of systems,
possibly satisfying additional conditions on the whole diagram.
System $\K$-embeddings form a category $\operatorname{Sys}_G(\K)$.
The scheme arrows select a subcategory on designated objects;
not every system $\K$-embedding is necessarily an arrow.
The working package requires that all system isomorphisms
are arrows and that arrows compose.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Many-sorted coding and sorted tuples]
For each $v\in V$, introduce a separate sort $S_v$
with a copy of the language $\tau$. For each $e:v\to w$,
include a function symbol $F_e:S_v\to S_w$ interpreted by
$f_e^\mathcal M$; name distinguished data in their own sorts.
A \emph{sorted tuple} is $\ba=(a_i)_{i<\gamma}$
with a specified sort $v_i\in V$ and $a_i\in M_{v_i}$
for each $i<\gamma$. Its atomic diagram is computed
in the resulting many-sorted language.
Coincident underlying elements in different vertices
are distinct sorted occurrences.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Structural versus strong information]
An embedding of many-sorted structures need not be
a $\K$-embedding of systems: strongness is generally
not expressible in their atomic structure.
Even a componentwise strong system embedding need
not be an arrow of a particular working scheme.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Eligible levels, presentations, and comparisons}
\begin{definition}[Eligible levels and bounded lists]
Fix an unbounded definable class $\mathcal S$ of infinite
cardinals, the \emph{eligible levels}. A system is at level
$\mu\in\mathcal S$ if all vertices have size $\mu$.
A sorted list is \emph{bounded at $\mu$} if its length
is less than $\mu$. Strict closure under a bound
$u$ means $u(\theta)<\mu$ for each infinite $\theta<\mu$.
These conventions follow \cite[Convention 5.1]{oai-ecc}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Finite-support presentation template]
Fix a set of term labels, each of a fixed vertex sort
and finite arity. A \emph{presentation template} $F$
assigns a $G$-system $F(I)$ to each eligible well-order $I$.
Each vertex occurrence has a representation
\[
a=t(i_0,\ldots,i_{n-1}),\qquad
i_0<\cdots<i_{n-1}\text{ in }I,
\]
with label $t$ of the appropriate sort.
Equality, vertex relations and operations, and edge maps
are evaluated uniformly from term labels and the finite
order/equality patterns of skeleton coordinates.
Distinct term representations may yield the same occurrence.
We separate this presentation data from the functoriality
and representation axioms imposed below.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Diagram comparisons]
For each sorted index sequence
$\mathbf v=(v_i)_{i<\gamma}$ of length bounded
at the levels considered, a \emph{diagram comparison}
is an equivalence relation $\equiv_D^{\mathbf v}$
on pairs $(\ba;\mathcal M)$, where $\mathcal M$ is
an eligible system and $\ba$ is a list of those sorts.
We use the diagram-class notation
\[
D(\ba;\mathcal M)=D(\bb;\mathcal N)
\iff
(\ba;\mathcal M)\equiv_D^{\mathbf v}(\bb;\mathcal N).
\]
No preassigned set of diagram codes is required.
Invariance under arrows, realization, and characterization
by isomorphisms are separate working-package axioms.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Possible joint diagram]
For a bounded actual sorted list $\ba$ in $\mathcal M$,
a diagram $q(\bd,\ba)$ is \emph{possible over $\ba$}
if some eligible system $\mathcal N$ has a joint
list $(\bd',\ba')$ of bounded length satisfying
\[
D(\bd',\ba';\mathcal N)=q,\qquad
D(\ba';\mathcal N)=D(\ba;\mathcal M).
\]
It need not initially be realized with the
actual parameters $\ba$ fixed in $\mathcal M$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Axioms of a working scheme}
\begin{definition}[Working package;
{\cite[Definition 5.2]{oai-ecc}}]\label{def:working-scheme}
A \emph{working scheme} consists of eligible
classes $\mathcal C_\mu$ of level-$\mu$ systems,
a finite-support template $F$, designated
system arrows (including mixed-level arrows),
and the diagram comparisons above, satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(W\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Structural closure.}
Arrows include all system isomorphisms, compose,
and are invariant under isomorphisms.
If $\mathcal X\subseteq\mathcal Y\subseteq\mathcal Z$
and both inclusions into $\mathcal Z$ are arrows,
then $\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y$ is an arrow.
An increasing nonempty chain of level-$\mu$
objects with componentwise union of total
cardinality $\mu$ has its union as an object
at level $\mu$, with inclusion arrows.
The union also includes by an arrow into
any common eligible upper object into which
all chain members include by arrows.

\item \textbf{Presentations and downward closure.}
For every eligible well-order $I$ of size $\mu$,
$F(I)\in\mathcal C_\mu$. Order embeddings
between eligible well-orders induce arrows,
including at mixed levels.
Every level-$\mu$ object is isomorphic
to some $F(I)$ with $|I|=\mu$.
For $\nu\leq\mu$ eligible, any set of
at most $\nu$ vertex occurrences in a
level-$\mu$ object, together with required
distinguished data, lies in a level-$\nu$
subobject whose inclusion is an arrow.

\item \textbf{Diagram invariance and homogeneity.}
Diagrams include the atomic diagram of the
entire many-sorted code and respect
restrictions and reindexing.
Every arrow, including mixed-level arrows,
preserves diagrams. For two bounded lists
at the same level, equality of diagrams
is equivalent to the existence of a
system isomorphism identifying the lists.
At each fixed sorted arity, the number
of diagrams is bounded by a set independent
of the eligible level.
Every possible joint diagram extending the
diagram of an actual bounded list is realized
over that very list at its level, whenever
the joint length is bounded there.
On represented term lists, diagrams depend
only on labels and joint order/equality patterns
of the skeleton positions, even at mixed levels.

\item \textbf{Exhaustion criterion.}
Compatible diagram matches on increasing
bounded lists that exhaust the source system
define an arrow, provided each source
vertex is exhausted by nested enumerations
of strong submodels of size below the
source level, and the image of each such
enumeration is a strong submodel of the
corresponding target vertex.
Any further scheme-specific arrow condition
must be proved independently in that construction.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Definitions versus hypotheses]
The presentation and comparison structures above
are data. Conditions (W1)--(W4) are substantive
axioms, not consequences of the definitions.
In particular, the same-level isomorphism
characterization of diagrams is an assumption,
rather than a definition of the equivalence relation.
As in the source, the empty list has one diagram;
combined with (W3) this makes all objects at
a fixed eligible level isomorphic.
The extra obligation on geometric arrows is
not eliminated by checking componentwise strongness.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation of the working package to AEC machinery]
A system arrow is a commuting family of $\K$-embeddings,
possibly with additional global conditions. A many-sorted
$\tau(\K)$-embedding is weaker: it need not be strong
on each vertex. The template $F$ generalizes the
functorial finite-support EM construction from one
model to a finite diagram of models.

The equivalence relations called \emph{diagrams} in the
working package are primitive comparison data; the
axiom identifying diagram equality with an isomorphism
at a fixed level is a high degree of homogeneity, not
a formal consequence of the definition. The extension
axiom plays a role analogous to realizing compatible
Galois types over a fixed parameter list. These are
\emph{analogies}: the package does not stipulate that
its comparisons are Galois types, nor does it
assume amalgamation for every constituent model.
\end{remark}
